Effective November 13, 2026 · Version 1.1

For parents. NetNest helps you limit what children can open on your home Wi-Fi. You must be an adult who controls that network. Filtering runs on a box in your house. If that box is off, lookups that depend on it stop.

2. Who may use NetNest

You must be at least 18, or the age of majority where you live. You confirm that:

  • You are a parent, guardian, or other adult allowed to manage the children and devices on this network.
  • You control the home router, or the person who controls it has agreed to point its DNS at NetNest One.
  • You will not use NetNest to watch people or devices you are not allowed to manage.

3. How the product works

  • NetNest One. The box in your home decides which DNS lookups to block. Child profiles, device names, rules, and bedtime schedules stay on that box.
  • Parent app. The phone signs you in, talks to the box on your home Wi-Fi, and keeps a local copy so screens can open when you are away.
  • Parent account. Sign-in (email or Google) identifies you. It does not store your household’s DNS history.
  • Router DNS. Blocking on other devices works when the router sends DNS to NetNest One. If both router DNS addresses are the box, those lookups fail while the box is unplugged. The internet line itself is unchanged.
  • Not a complete block. Private DNS, a VPN, mobile data, or a hand-typed DNS address can skip the box. NetNest does not claim to stop every app.
